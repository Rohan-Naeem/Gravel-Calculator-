Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions we hear most often about calculating gravel.

How do I calculate how much gravel I need?

Measure the length, width, and depth of your project area, then use the gravel calculator to convert those measurements into cubic feet and cubic yards. See the formula page for the exact math.

How many cubic yards of gravel do I need?

Multiply length, width, and depth in feet to get cubic feet, then divide by 27 to get cubic yards. The calculator performs this conversion automatically once you enter your measurements.

How deep should gravel be?

Depth depends on the project. Decorative or top-dressing layers are typically shallower, while structural base layers under driveways or patios are usually deeper. Check guidance for your specific project type or ask a local supplier.

Why should I add extra gravel?

Gravel settles over time, some material is lost during spreading, and irregular edges rarely match a perfect rectangle. Adding a waste percentage — commonly 5% to 20% — helps avoid running short.

Is a cubic yard the same as a ton?

No. A cubic yard is a measure of volume, while a ton is a measure of weight. The relationship between the two depends on the density of the specific gravel, which varies by material and moisture content.

Can I enter measurements in inches?

Yes. The calculator supports feet, meters, inches, and centimeters. Select your preferred unit from the dropdown before calculating.

Does gravel weight vary?

Yes. Weight depends on the type of stone, how tightly it's packed, and how much moisture it holds. The same volume of two different gravel types can weigh noticeably different amounts.

What is the difference between gravel volume and gravel weight?

Volume describes the space the gravel occupies, measured in cubic feet or cubic yards. Weight describes how heavy that gravel is, measured in pounds or tons. This calculator estimates volume; for weight, you'll need the density of your chosen material.
