Gravel Guide

How to measure, calculate, and order gravel with confidence.

How to Measure a Gravel Area

Start by measuring the length and width of the space in a straight line, using a tape measure or measuring wheel for larger areas. For irregular shapes, break the area into rectangles, measure each one, and add the results together. Round measurements to the nearest inch or centimeter for accuracy.

How to Choose a Depth

Depth depends on what the gravel is for. A decorative top layer over landscaping fabric is usually shallower than a structural base beneath a driveway or patio. If you're unsure what depth your project calls for, ask your local supplier or check guidance specific to your project type before finalizing your order.

How to Calculate Volume

Once you have length, width, and depth in the same unit, multiply them together to get cubic feet. The gravel calculator handles this automatically, including converting between feet, meters, inches, and centimeters.

Converting Cubic Feet to Cubic Yards

Most suppliers price and deliver gravel by the cubic yard rather than the cubic foot. Divide your cubic feet total by 27 to get cubic yards. See the formula page for the full calculation and a worked example.

Why Extra Material May Be Needed

Real-world gravel spreading rarely matches a perfect calculation. Some material is lost to uneven ground, spillage, or trimming edges, and gravel settles further once it's driven or walked on. Adding a waste allowance — commonly 5% to 20% — helps you avoid a second trip to the supplier.

How Gravel Settling Affects Estimates

Loose gravel takes up more space than gravel that has been compacted by weight or vibration. Over time, and especially under vehicle traffic, a layer can settle and appear thinner than when it was first spread. This is one of several reasons calculator results should be treated as a starting estimate rather than a final figure.

Why Actual Quantities Vary

Density differs by material, moisture content changes weight, and site conditions such as slope or uneven subgrade all affect how much gravel is actually used. Two projects with identical dimensions can require different amounts of material depending on these factors.

Discussing Your Order with a Supplier

When you contact a gravel supplier, share your calculated cubic yardage along with the type of gravel and its intended use. Suppliers can advise on typical depth recommendations for your project type and may round up to the nearest standard load size. It's reasonable to ask about delivery minimums, bulk pricing, and whether the quoted volume is loose or compacted.
