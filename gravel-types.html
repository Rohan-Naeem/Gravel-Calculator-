Common Gravel Types

An overview of the aggregate materials most often used in landscaping and construction.

Pea Gravel

Small, naturally rounded stones roughly the size of a pea, typically light in color with mixed earthy tones.

Common Uses
Walkways, garden beds, playgrounds, and decorative ground cover.
General Characteristics
Smooth and rounded, comfortable underfoot, and tends to shift more than angular stone since the rounded pieces don't lock together as tightly.

Crushed Stone

Mechanically crushed rock with angular, jagged edges, available in a range of sizes graded by number.

Common Uses
Driveways, road base, drainage layers, and structural fill beneath patios or pavers.
General Characteristics
Angular pieces interlock well, creating a stable, compactable base that resists shifting under weight.

River Rock

Naturally smooth, rounded stones shaped over time by moving water, usually larger than pea gravel.

Common Uses
Decorative landscaping, dry creek beds, drainage channels, and accent borders.
General Characteristics
Smooth surface and rounded shape, often sold in mixed sizes for a natural, varied appearance.

Limestone

A sedimentary rock, typically light gray to off-white, crushed into gravel form for construction use.

Common Uses
Driveways, base layers, and general fill where a compactable, cost-effective material is needed.
General Characteristics
Compacts well when crushed and graded, and its lighter color is a common aesthetic choice for open driveway areas.

Other Common Aggregates

Many regions also offer locally quarried aggregates such as granite gravel, decomposed granite, or recycled concrete aggregate.

Common Uses
Regional and specialty applications, from decomposed granite paths to recycled aggregate base fill.
General Characteristics
Appearance and performance vary by source material and region, so it's worth asking a local supplier what's available near you.

Exact density and weight vary by quarry, moisture content, and gradation. Ask your supplier for material-specific weight figures if you need a precise tonnage rather than a volume estimate.
