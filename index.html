What Is a Gravel Calculator?

A gravel calculator is a simple planning tool that turns the dimensions of a project area into an estimated volume of gravel. Instead of guessing how many bags or truckloads to order, you enter the length, width, and depth of the space you plan to cover, and the calculator converts those measurements into square footage and cubic volume.

This is useful whether you are covering a driveway, laying a garden path, or building up a base layer under a patio. The calculator does the arithmetic — including unit conversion and an allowance for waste or settling — so you can walk into a supplier with a clearer idea of the quantity to ask for.

How to Calculate Gravel

Three simple steps take you from a bare patch of ground to a usable estimate.

  1. 1

    Measure the Area

    Measure the length and width of the space you want to cover. Round to the nearest convenient unit and note irregular edges.

  2. 2

    Choose the Depth

    Decide how deep the gravel layer needs to be. Depth requirements vary by project type, from a thin decorative layer to a compacted base.

  3. 3

    Calculate the Volume

    Multiply length, width, and depth to get cubic feet, then convert to cubic yards and add a waste allowance for ordering.

What the Calculator Provides

Each calculation returns four figures you can use when planning or ordering material.

Area

The total surface area of your project in square feet, based on length and width.

Cubic Feet

The base volume of gravel required, calculated from your area and chosen depth.

Cubic Yards

Cubic feet converted to cubic yards, the unit most suppliers use for bulk pricing.

Extra Material

Your chosen waste percentage applied to the total, so you don't come up short on site.

Frequently Asked Questions

A few quick answers before you calculate.

How many cubic yards of gravel do I need?

Multiply the length, width, and depth of your area in feet to get cubic feet, then divide by 27. The calculator above does this automatically.

How deep should a gravel layer be?

Depth depends on the project — decorative beds are often shallow, while driveways and bases are typically deeper. Enter your planned depth into the calculator.

Why does the calculator add extra percentage?

Gravel settles and compacts over time, and some material is lost during spreading. A waste allowance helps you avoid ordering short.
